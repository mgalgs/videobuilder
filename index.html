Classic principles

Anticipation
Animation principles / Classic principles · anticipation

Just before a big move, the object eases a little the opposite way, as if gathering itself, then springs off fast in the real direction.

Appeal
Animation principles / Classic principles · appeal

Motion given enough character, through clear shapes, lively timing and a hint of emotion, that the viewer enjoys watching it and warms to whatever is moving.

Arcs
Animation principles / Classic principles · arcs

Movement from one point to another follows a gentle curve instead of a straight line, which makes it feel natural and alive rather than mechanical.

Anticipation, squash and stretch, and follow-through.

Exaggeration

Every part of a move is pushed well past what is realistic, a deeper crouch, a bigger leap, a harder squash, so the action reads instantly and with more energy.

Follow Through
Animation principles / Classic principles · follow-through

When a moving thing comes to a stop, its loose bits (a tail, hair, an antenna) carry on past it, swing back and settle a beat later.

Secondary Action
Animation principles / Classic principles · secondary-action

A smaller movement layered over the main one, such as a wave while walking, that adds personality without stopping or competing with the main movement.

Slow In and Slow Out
Animation principles / Classic principles · slow-in-and-slow-out

A move that gathers speed gently from rest and gently sheds it again before stopping, so most of its frames sit near the two ends and few in the fast middle.

Solid Drawing
Animation principles / Classic principles · solid-drawing

An object keeps the same size, proportions and perspective as it turns and travels, so every angle shows one solid thing with real weight and depth.

Squash and Stretch
Animation principles / Classic principles · squash-and-stretch

A moving body grows long and thin along its path while it travels fast and goes short and wide when it lands, trading width for height so it seems to hold the same amount of stuff.

Staging
Animation principles / Classic principles · staging

The frame is arranged so one subject clearly owns it, with everything else pushed back in brightness, size and placement, so the eye goes straight to the action that matters.

Timing
Animation principles / Classic principles · timing

Choosing how many frames a move lasts so its speed tells the viewer how heavy, light, urgent or relaxed the thing moving is.
